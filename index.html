Research

We develop AI for Life Engineering. Our research investigates the extent to which an individual's behavior can be computationally modeled and reproduced across scales, contexts, and over time. These efforts involve building AI models to characterize the mechanisms underlying individual behavior and enable its faithful emulation.

Biography

I joined Tsinghua University as a tenure-track assistant professor and principal investigator in biomedical AI. Previously, I was a postdoctoral fellow at Harvard University with Professor Pranav Rajpurkar. I obtained my Ph.D. in Computer Science from the University of Hong Kong, advised by Professor Yizhou Yu. I spent two years at Tencent as a research scientist, and received degrees from Nanjing University and Wuhan University. I also served as the founding member and head of AI systems at a2z Radiology AI.

AI for Medicine at Scale (AIMS) Lab

Evolution over time is a defining feature of life. Diseases progress, cells respond to perturbations, and individuals continually shape their behavior through accumulated experience. Yet most existing AI systems are trained on static, isolated data and struggle to capture how living systems change over time.

Our group has focused on AI for Life Engineering, organized around a shared scientific question: continual learning and dynamic state modeling from temporal multimodal data. We study how AI can keep updating its representation of a system’s internal state as new data arrive, and how that representation can support prediction, decision–making, and intervention. This agenda comprises the following research directions:

  1. Computational models of the dynamic states of the human body and their evolution.

    Drawing on longitudinal medical data and patient trajectories, we learn dynamic representations of patient states and the mechanisms by which they evolve. These models predict how external interventions affect physiological states and health outcomes, enabling prospective simulation, comparison, and optimization of alternative intervention strategies. We systematically validate model predictions through retrospective and prospective clinical studies, with the aim of providing a reliable basis for individualized precision medicine.

  2. From analytical tool to autonomous scientific agent.

    We aim to transform AI from a tool that assists with data analysis into an active agent of scientific discovery. To this end, we are building biomedical autonomous laboratories that integrate computational modeling, hypothesis generation, experimental design, robotic execution, and multimodal measurement, progressively enabling accurate prediction of responses to perturbations, targeted optimization of biological states and functions, and autonomous discovery of biological mechanisms.

  3. Enminded AI.

    The mind is shaped by experience and, in turn, drives behavior. Building on long–term, real–world data from individuals, we investigate how experience shapes the internal states that drive cognition, decision–making, and behavior, and we probe the limits to which individual behavior can be computationally modeled and reproduced. From these insights, we develop agents and robotic systems that continually accumulate experience and keep evolving through interaction with people and the physical world.
